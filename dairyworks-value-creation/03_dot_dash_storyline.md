====================================================================
GOVERNING THOUGHT (answer first)
====================================================================
• Dairyworks can roughly QUADRUPLE EBITDA to ~AED 445M over five years by reversing a
  self-inflicted, share-losing decline — the plan is credible, mostly within management
  control, and largely self-funding.
  – Today: AED 1,900M revenue but only AED 111M EBITDA (5.8%) — vs 18.1% four years ago.
  – Prize: ~AED 2,700M revenue and ~AED 445M EBITDA (16.5%, base case) at Year 5.
  – Why believable: peers earn 17–27%; the markets are growing ~5–8%/yr; the biggest
    lever (distribution) is operational self-help, not capital-intensive.

====================================================================
S — SITUATION (where we are)
====================================================================
• Dairyworks is a scaled, 30-year-old UAE dairy player — but it is losing ground on
  every dimension that matters.
  – #2 in UAE (~15% share); milk, yogurt, ice cream across UAE, KSA, Qatar.
  – Three UAE plants; distribution in-house in UAE, outsourced in KSA/Qatar.
  – Weak brand: only 1-in-5 consumers recognize it; 1-in-10 actively seek it (2019 Nielsen).

====================================================================
C — COMPLICATION (why we must act)
====================================================================
• EBITDA collapsed from AED 362M to AED 111M (18.1% → 5.8%) — a −69% fall, −25.6%/yr.
  – Near break-even: EBIT margin hit 0.6% in 2021 — one bad year from losing money.

• It is a SHARE-LOSS DOOM-LOOP, not just a margin problem. [Appendix A–B]
  – Volume fell −13% (313→272kt) while the UAE market grew; price (+10%) masked it.
  – That masked volume loss detonated cost-to-serve: S&D PER TON rose +61% (deleverage).
  – Fixing S&D and winning back volume are therefore the SAME lever, not two.

• Two controllable lines explain the entire 12.3pp collapse — but one is the real culprit.
  – S&D +7.9pp (the biggest single destroyer, −AED 134M) and COGS +5.2pp.
  – A&M stuck at 0.6% vs peers' 2–3% — demand starved while over-paying to supply.

• Peer normalization proves COGS is exogenous and S&D is self-inflicted. [Appendix E — key reframe]
  – Every peer's COGS rose (Almarai +10.3pp, worse than us) → input inflation hit everyone.
  – Every peer HELD or IMPROVED S&D; only Dairyworks blew it up (+7.9pp).
  – So ~half the margin loss is industry-wide; ~half is self-inflicted S&D — and fully fixable.

• The market is a tailwind we are forfeiting. [Appendix B–D]
  – UAE/KSA/Qatar growing ~5–8%/yr; forward UAE market accelerates to ~7%/yr.
  – Share loss is concentrated: we held Yogurt (+4.2%) but lost Milk (−1.7%) and Ice cream (−9.5%).
  – We are abandoning Ice cream — our HIGHEST-margin category (40% GP) — into a reaccelerating market.
  – The bleed is the UAE home market (−3%/yr); GCC export held flat-to-up. [Appendix C]

====================================================================
R — RESOLUTION (what we do about it)
====================================================================
• Full potential: ~AED 2,700M revenue and ~AED 445M EBITDA at Year 5 — most of the
  prize is capturing growth we currently forfeit.
  – Revenue bridge: +market capture (~AED 600M) + win share (~AED 140M) + adjacencies (~AED 60M).
  – EBITDA bridge: +8.4pp S&D turnaround, +5.0pp gross-margin (cost+mix, net), −2.7pp brand reinvest.
  – Range: 12.5% downside / 16.5% base / 18.3% upside → ~AED 340–495M; even downside ≈ 3× FY21. [Appendix F]

• Four initiatives turn the diagnosis into value — a coherent, self-funding system.
  – 1. DISTRIBUTION TURNAROUND (largest lever): renegotiate/insource KSA & Qatar; rebuild UAE RTM.
  – 2. COST & MANUFACTURING: modernize the 3 UAE plants; centralize procurement; capture scale.
  – 3. PORTFOLIO PREMIUMIZATION: scale organic milk & flavored yogurt; relaunch ice cream; prune the tail.
  – 4. BRAND & DEMAND RESET: lift A&M 0.6%→3.3% (challenger level) to fix awareness and pull volume.

• A three-horizon roadmap — each horizon self-funds the next.
  – H1 (0–12m) Stop the bleed: distribution renegotiation, procurement quick wins, SKU cuts, brand brief.
  – H2 (12–36m) Build the engine: plant capex, premium scale-up, KSA share offensive, national campaign.
  – H3 (36–60m) Full potential: adjacencies (plant-based/functional), channel/online leadership, ~16.5%.

• It needs capital and carries real risk — but it is largely self-funding and beats inaction.
  – Capex ~AED 200–350M (plants, Yrs 2–4); working capital ~AED 80–120M; brand step-up to ~AED 90M/yr.
  – Self-help frees ~AED 50–70M/yr from Year 1; peak external need ~AED 100–150M; payback <4 years.
  – Risks: Almarai competitive response; input/energy inflation & FX (exogenous); execution on insourcing & capex.
