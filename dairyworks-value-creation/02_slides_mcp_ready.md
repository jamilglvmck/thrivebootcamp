## SLIDE 1: The prize is AED 384M of EBITDA — and it is ours to take back

### Layout
Archetype: scr_dark_callout
Template: executive_dashboard_4zone

### Encoding
stat_callouts

### Title
Dairyworks can roughly 4.5x EBITDA to ~AED 495M by 2026 — the decline is self-inflicted and reversible

### Subtitle
A four-initiative value creation plan in markets that are growing 5–8% per year

### Governing Thought
Margins collapsed 12 points while Dairyworks' markets grew 5–8% per year; fixing distribution and cost, reinvesting in brand, and tilting to premium/health restores peer-level economics.

### Situation and complication
- Dairyworks is the #2 dairy player in the UAE (~15% share) with AED 1,900M revenue across milk, yogurt and ice cream in UAE, KSA and Qatar
- EBITDA has fallen 69% — from AED 362M (18.1% margin) in 2017 to AED 111M (5.8%) in 2021 — a −25.6% annual decline
- The cause is internal: S&D costs rose +7.9pp and COGS +5.2pp of revenue, while brand investment stayed starved at 0.6% vs peers' 2–3%

### The opportunity
- Full potential: Revenue ~AED 2,700M (+7.3% CAGR) and EBITDA ~AED 495M (18.3% margin) by 2026
- Uplift: +AED 384M EBITDA (~4.5x) and +AED 800M revenue versus 2021
- Peers prove it is achievable: Almarai 27%, NADEC 19%, SADAFCO 17% EBITDA margin today

### The ask
- Approve the 5-year value creation plan and its four initiatives, and fund a Year-1 distribution and brand reset to stop the decline

### Source
Dairyworks P&L 2017–2021; peer P&Ls (Almarai, NADEC, SADAFCO); Euromonitor 2021–2026; team analysis.

## SLIDE 2: Dairyworks is a scaled but eroding franchise with structural self-help levers

### Layout
Archetype: zone_layout
Template: zone_3_T

### Encoding
icons + stat_callouts

### Title
A 30-year-old, three-category business across three GCC markets — scaled, but losing ground on every dimension

### Subtitle
Current-state snapshot: portfolio, footprint, channels and brand

### Governing Thought
Dairyworks has real scale and a broad footprint, but a weak brand, an inefficient cost base and a commoditized core are eroding its position.

### Portfolio and footprint
- Founded 1990; UAE-based; mass-market positioning across three categories — milk, yogurt and ice cream
- Five core SKUs spanning value to premium: standard milk (AED 4.5/L), organic milk (AED 9/L), standard yogurt (AED 3.5/500g), flavored yogurt (AED 1.5/120g), ice cream (AED 10/L)
- 3 manufacturing facilities, all in the UAE; distribution in-house in UAE, outsourced in KSA and Qatar

### Channels
- Offline modern retail ~65% and traditional retail ~20% dominate the mix; online full-basket ~10% and quick-commerce replenishment ~5% are small but fast-growing
- Distribution issues reported across all three countries — a recurring theme in the cost base

### Brand health (2019 Nielsen, n=1,000)
- Only 1 in 5 consumers recognize the Dairyworks brand
- ~60% of those who know it say they do not understand the brand
- Only 1 in 10 consumers actively seek out Dairyworks products

### Implications / Callout
Dairyworks has the assets to win — scale, footprint, a premium tail — but brand, cost and distribution are all working against it. Every one of these is a self-help lever.

### Source
Dairyworks fact-pack (introduction, product portfolio, footprint & channels); 2019 Nielsen consumer survey (n=1,000).

## SLIDE 3: EBITDA has fallen 69% in four years — this is a burning platform

### Layout
Archetype: chart_with_breakdown
Template: 2_3_dark
Chart: line_chart (EBITDA margin %, 2017–2021) + bar overlay (EBITDA AED mn)

### Encoding
line_chart + phase_colors

### Title
EBITDA collapsed from AED 362M to AED 111M and margin fell from 18.1% to 5.8% — a −25.6% annual decline

### Subtitle
Dairyworks P&L trajectory, 2017–2021

### Governing Thought
The business is approaching break-even on EBIT (AED 12M in 2021), so inaction is not an option — value is being destroyed every year.

### EBITDA trajectory (AED mn and % margin)
| Year | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (AED mn) | 2,000 | 2,020 | 2,010 | 1,960 | 1,900 |
| EBITDA (AED mn) | 362 | 372 | 307 | 232 | 111 |
| EBITDA margin | 18.1% | 18.4% | 15.3% | 11.9% | 5.8% |

### What is happening
- Revenue is roughly flat (−1.3% CAGR) but EBITDA fell 69% — the problem is margin, not top line
- Volume declined −3.5% per year (313kt to 272kt); Dairyworks offset this with +2.3%/yr price increases, masking share loss
- By 2021 EBIT is effectively zero (0.6% margin) — the franchise is one bad year from losing money

### Implications / Callout
At 5.8% EBITDA, Dairyworks earns less than a quarter of Almarai's margin. Every year of delay compounds the value destroyed.

### Source
Dairyworks P&L 2017–2021 (volume, revenue, EBITDA, EBIT).

### Encoding

### Title

### Subtitle
EBITDA margin bridge, 2017 to 2021 (% of revenue)

### Governing Thought
Because the margin loss is concentrated in distribution and manufacturing cost — both operational — it can be reversed with management action.

### Cost walk (% of revenue)
| Line | 2017 | 2021 | Change (pp) | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COGS | 59.3% | 64.5% | +5.2 worse | HIGH |

## SLIDE 9: Four initiatives turn the diagnosis into value

### Layout
Archetype: zone_layout
Template: zone_4_dashboard

### Encoding
icons + numbered_bars + stat_callouts

### Title
A coherent system: fix the cost and distribution base, reinvest in brand, and tilt the portfolio to premium/health

### Subtitle
The four strategic initiatives and their primary value lever

### Governing Thought
The initiatives are sequenced and mutually reinforcing — cost and distribution fund the brand and premium investment that drives growth.

### 1. Distribution turnaround (largest EBITDA lever)
- Renegotiate or insource the outsourced KSA and Qatar networks; rebuild UAE route-to-market and win back lost shelf
- Target: S&D from 24.9% to ~16.5% of revenue (+8.4pp EBITDA)

### 2. Cost & manufacturing modernization
- Upgrade the three UAE plants, centralize procurement, and capture economies of scale
- Target: COGS from 64.5% to ~60.0% of revenue (+4.5pp EBITDA)

### 3. Portfolio premiumization
- Scale organic milk and flavored yogurt; rationalize loss-making commodity SKUs; enter plant-based and functional dairy
- Target: +AED 360M revenue and +1.5pp blended margin

### 4. Brand & demand reset
- Step up A&M from 0.6% to 2.5% to fix 1-in-5 awareness and pull premium volume
- Target: enables the full revenue bridge; −1.9pp margin investment with high ROI

### Implications / Callout
No single initiative is sufficient — together they move Dairyworks from 5.8% to 18.3% EBITDA and from decline to 7% growth.

### Source
Team synthesis of diagnostic findings; Dairyworks cost structure and portfolio data; peer benchmark.

### Subtitle

### Governing Thought
Front-loading distribution and procurement self-help generates the cash to fund brand and plant investment without external capital.

### Horizon 1 — Stop the bleed (0–12 months)
- Renegotiate KSA & Qatar distribution contracts; procurement quick wins; rationalize loss-making commodity SKUs; brand relaunch brief
- Owner: COO (distribution), CPO (procurement), CMO (brand) — Milestone: S&D down 3pp, break-even protected by Month 12

### Horizon 2 — Build the engine (12–36 months)
- Plant modernization capex; scale organic milk & flavored yogurt; KSA share offensive; national brand campaign
- Owner: COO / CMO — Milestone: EBITDA margin to ~12%, KSA share 2%→4% by Month 36

### Horizon 3 — Reach full potential (36–60 months)
- Enter plant-based & functional dairy; lead modern & online channels; embed premium mix
- Owner: CEO / CSO — Milestone: Revenue ~AED 2,700M, EBITDA ~18.3% by Year 5

### Implications / Callout
Each horizon is self-funding: Horizon 1 savings finance Horizon 2 investment, which finances Horizon 3 growth.
