Situation · Current state
2

A 30-year-old, three-category business across three GCC markets — scaled, but losing ground on every dimension

Portfolio, footprint, channels and brand health
Portfolio & footprint
  • Founded 1990, UAE-based, mass-market across milk, yogurt and ice cream
  • 5 core SKUs from value to premium (milk AED 4.5–9/L, yogurt, ice cream AED 10/L)
  • 3 manufacturing plants, all in the UAE
  • Distribution in-house in UAE; outsourced in KSA and Qatar
Channels
  • Modern retail ~65%, traditional ~20% dominate
  • Online full-basket ~10%, quick-commerce ~5% — small but fast-growing
  • Distribution issues reported across all three countries
Brand health — 2019 Nielsen (n=1,000)
  • Only 1 in 5 consumers recognize the brand
  • ~60% of those who know it don't understand it
  • Only 1 in 10 actively seek out Dairyworks
Implication: Dairyworks has the assets to win — scale, footprint, a premium tail — but brand, cost and distribution all work against it. Every one is a self-help lever.
Source: Dairyworks fact-pack (introduction, portfolio, footprint & channels); 2019 Nielsen survey (n=1,000).

Why each line moved — controllable vs exogenous
  • S&D +7.9pp: part true inefficiency (outsourced KSA/Qatar), part operating deleverage — fixed cost spread over volume down 3.5%/yr. Both cured by the turnaround + volume recovery
  • COGS +5.2pp: outdated plants, no scale, weak procurement — but partly global dairy-input/energy inflation (exogenous); controllable share ~3–4pp
  • A&M flat at 0.6%: chronic brand under-investment vs peers' 2–3% — an under-spend to reverse, not a cost to cut
So what: The majority of the 12.3pp loss is controllable — distribution, scale and brand. Closing these moves Dairyworks toward the peer range; some COGS drag is exogenous inflation.

What it takes · Investment, cash & risk
11

The plan needs capital and carries real risk — but it is largely self-funding and the downside still beats inaction

Preliminary — capex, working capital and key risks to be sized in diligence
Investment required (5-year, preliminary)
  • Plant modernization capex: ~AED 200–350M across the 3 UAE facilities, phased over Years 2–4
  • Working capital: ~AED 80–120M to fund +42% revenue (fresh-dairy receivables + inventory)
  • Brand step-up: A&M from ~AED 11M to ~AED 90M/yr (0.6%→3.3%)
Funding & cash — largely self-funding
  • Self-help first: S&D + procurement quick wins free ~AED 50–70M/yr from Year 1, funding most of the ramp
  • Peak external need: ~AED 100–150M (phased), concentrated in the Year 2–3 capex peak
  • Payback target: <4 years on modernization; EBITDA +AED 335M by Year 5
Key risks & downside case
  • Competitive response: Almarai (40% KSA share, 27% margin) will defend — assume trade-spend/price retaliation; KSA target tempered to 2%→3.5%
  • Exogenous: dairy-input & energy inflation, FX — part of the COGS drag is outside management control
  • Execution: distribution insourcing and plant capex carry delivery risk
  • Downside case: partial S&D fix + slow brand ramp → ~12.5% margin / ~AED 340M (still 3× FY21)
Strategic choices & "why now"
  • Price → volume: reverse the price-led strategy (+2.3%/yr masking −3.5% volume); invest price to defend commodity, carry pricing on premium
  • KSA route-to-market: resolve make-vs-buy (insource vs renegotiate) — the binary behind +AED 140M of share gain
  • Why now / why succeed: new CEO mandate; levers are operational and peer-proven; low-risk self-help is front-loaded and benchmarked
Source: Team value creation model (preliminary); peer benchmark; capex/working-capital to be confirmed in diligence.
